# observer-conditioned settlement mapping

## owner view

- change: draft and calibrate a secondary analytical pass that asks what an
  explicitly bounded interactional projection treats as established, binding,
  authorized, or operative, and how those statuses persist, couple, or release.
- before: the programme draft proposes “truth-register coding,” which risks
  treating evidence, rules, roles, and goals as commensurate ontological bins
  and obscuring the observer's position inside the instrument.
- after: interactional hysteresis retains its temporal trace; a separate,
  optional settlement map can describe visible load-bearing statuses without
  claiming hidden authorship, hidden mechanism, or truth throughout the
  machine.
- authority: this is a draft planning artifact. acceptance of this exact
  revision would authorize slice 00 only: method-note drafting, a field
  template, and bounded calibration traces. it would not authorize the
  three-case result analysis, report claims, provider calls, publication,
  submission, commit, push, or disclosure of private source material.

## context

the first programme draft grouped evidence, rules, roles, goals, and their
coupling under “truth-register coding.” owner review marked that capability
“needs talk.” discussion exposed two problems.

first, the proposed categories answer different questions. evidence concerns
what is treated as established; rules concern what is treated as binding;
roles concern whose move is treated as authorized; goals concern what remains
operative. they are not necessarily mutually exclusive codes or equivalent
types of truth.

second, any such judgment is conditioned by the analyst's access. in a
mediated or multi-agent system, the visible speaker may compress routing,
aggregation, retained state, reference resolution, and operation modality.
the larger observer instrument can be represented as time `t`, projection
`pi`, reference resolver `rho`, speaker attribution `alpha`, and operation
modality `mu`. the current study does not require reconstructing that complete
machinery, but it cannot pretend the machinery is absent.

the proposed response is a minimum field declaration plus a settlement map.
the declaration marks the instrument's edge. the map then makes bounded claims
about the visible interaction. claims about a mechanism behind that projection
require separate evidence.

## owner decisions requested

### 1. use settlement mapping as a secondary pass

**recommendation:** trace interactional hysteresis first, then use settlement
mapping only where it helps explain an inflection point, persistence, release,
or cross-status coupling.

**alternative:** assign one truth-register code to every utterance or event.

**consequence:** the recommendation reduces taxonomy-first coding and allows a
case to produce no settlement claim where the evidence does not support one.

### 2. begin with four settlement predicates

**recommendation:** provisionally ask what the declared projection treats as
`established`, `binding`, `authorized`, and `operative`.

**alternative:** retain evidence, rule, role, and goal as direct codes.

**consequence:** predicates describe interactional status rather than naming
four kinds of truth. one state may bear several predicates, and the initial
four may be revised or abandoned after calibration.

### 3. keep the observer declaration minimal

**recommendation:** require `slice`, `projection`, `attribution`, and
`occlusion`; retain `rho` and `mu` as extensions invoked only when reference
resolution or operation modality materially changes the claim.

**alternative:** require `t, pi, rho, alpha, mu` for every case.

**consequence:** the recommendation imports the observer problem without
letting a larger system-reconstruction project consume the sprint.

### 4. map state propositions and transitions, not isolated wording

**recommendation:** use one bounded state proposition as the unit, then record
the evidence for acquisition, persistence, coupling, reverse pressure,
release, and residue.

**alternative:** label every conversational turn independently.

**consequence:** the recommendation remains compatible with interactional
hysteresis and makes the ratchet relation visible without treating every
utterance as a self-contained state.

### 5. distinguish calibrated boundaries from evasive hedging

**recommendation:** require every uncertainty marker to identify the missing
observation that bounds the claim; treat new distinctions introduced only to
prevent adequate evidence from settling anything as candidate interactional
behavior, not methodological caution.

**alternative:** treat any added qualification as equivalent epistemic care.

**consequence:** the recommendation allows honest claim limitation without
reproducing the successor-distinction mechanism inside the method.

## scope

in:

- define the minimum observer declaration and its relation to the larger
  `t, pi, rho, alpha, mu` instrument;
- define provisional settlement predicates and non-exclusive use;
- define acquisition, persistence, coupling, reverse pressure, release, and
  residue for a bounded state proposition;
- specify evidence status and causal ceilings for every mapped movement;
- draft one compact field template;
- calibrate it on one owner-selected bounded trace and one boundary case;
- produce public-safe derived examples while keeping private wording and
  source locators outside tracked repository state; and
- record whether the construct should be retained, renamed, revised, or
  abandoned after calibration.

out:

- analysis of all three proposed result episodes;
- reconstruction or audit of pugswarm or another hidden multi-agent system;
- claims about model belief, intent, unitary speakerhood, or causal authorship;
- mandatory coding of every turn or forced use of all four predicates;
- treating absent observation as evidence of absence;
- final interactional-hysteresis v1.1 acceptance;
- report drafting, provider calls, evaluation, publication, submission,
  commit, push, deployment, or visibility change; and
- disclosure of raw chats, screenshots, exports, or identifying third-party
  material.

## governing contracts

1. the observer declaration conditions every settlement claim.
2. the visible or apparent speaker is not silently equated with causal author.
3. observations, analyst inferences, owner retrospection, and mechanism
   hypotheses remain separately marked.
4. settlement means stable enough for the interaction to proceed as though a
   state holds; it does not mean universally true, consensually believed, or
   permanently fixed.
5. a state may receive zero, one, or several settlement predicates.
6. cross-status coupling is recorded as a supported transition, not inferred
   merely because two statuses co-occur.
7. “not visible under this projection” remains distinct from “did not happen.”
8. calibration can falsify the usefulness of the proposed map.
9. private source wording remains outside tracked artifacts.
10. provisional review marks and visual interaction confer no acceptance.

## proposed method object

### observer declaration

| field | question | required? |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| slice | what interval and artifact boundary are under observation? | yes |
| projection | what portion or representation of the interaction is available for analysis? | yes |
| attribution | which apparent speaker or participant label is used, under what attribution policy? | yes |
| occlusion | what mediation, state, routing, or authorship remains unavailable? | yes |
| reference resolver | how are ambiguous entities or prior turns resolved when that choice affects the claim? | conditional |
| operation modality | through what operation or interface did the visible move occur when that modality affects the claim? | conditional |

### settlement predicates

| predicate | working question | non-claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| established | what does this projection treat as evidentially available or settled enough to reason from? | not universal truth or private belief |
| binding | what constraint actually governs the next available moves? | not merely a stated policy |
| authorized | whose move can alter interpretation, scope, or state? | not a complete account of causal control |
| operative | what objective does subsequent activity continue to optimize? | not necessarily the goal participants endorse aloud |

### movement record

each mapped movement records:

- one bounded state proposition;
- applicable settlement predicate or predicates;
- the first supporting observation and source status;
- acquisition or reinforcement event;
- later behavior that relies on the state;
- cross-status coupling, if supported;
- reverse pressure and whether it is received;
- release, bypass, exit, or unresolved persistence;
- residue after local repair;
- retained alternative explanations; and
- the observation that would discriminate among them.

## calibration design

### normal case

use one bounded passage where a proposed rule remains binding, an owner role is
nominally authorized, and inherited work nevertheless remains operative. test
whether the map adds explanatory value beyond the ordinary hysteresis trace.

### boundary case

use one short passage where a qualification properly limits a claim because a
specific observation is unavailable. test whether the method can distinguish
that boundary from a distinction introduced to prevent already-adequate
evidence from changing the interaction.

the owner selects the exact passages before calibration. private annotations
use opaque locators. the tracked repository receives only synthetic or
paraphrased traces approved for that purpose.

## implementation by invariant

### step 1 — method note

write the observer declaration, settlement predicates, movement record, and
claim ceiling without importing results from the selected cases.

**proof:** every field has a question, evidence expectation, non-claim, and
omission rule.

### step 2 — field template

create a compact private-case template that permits multiple predicates, no
predicate, and explicit “not visible” states.

**proof:** the template can represent both the normal and boundary calibration
cases without adding a new field mid-trace.

### step 3 — bounded calibration

apply the template to the two owner-selected passages and compare it with an
ordinary interactional-hysteresis trace.

**proof:** a calibration memo identifies added explanatory value, redundancy,
false precision, missed relations, and disagreements rather than merely
showing that the template can be filled.

### step 4 — disposition

present a recommendation to retain, rename, revise, or abandon the construct.

**proof:** the recommendation cites the calibration evidence and preserves the
owner's contrary readings.

## acceptance criteria

1. the observer declaration identifies slice, projection, attribution, and
   occlusion before any settlement claim.
2. conditional resolver and modality fields are invoked only when they alter
   the claim.
3. the map permits zero, one, or multiple predicates for one state proposition.
4. every mapped movement cites an observable support and evidence status.
5. cross-status coupling requires evidence of one status affecting another,
   not mere co-occurrence.
6. “not visible,” “not observed,” and “absent” remain distinct.
7. the boundary calibration distinguishes a claim-limiting hedge from a
   candidate successor distinction by naming the missing or already-satisfied
   observation.
8. calibration records redundancy and false precision as possible failures.
9. no result claim from the three proposed episodes is promoted under this
   slice.
10. tracked and generated artifacts contain no raw private source text or
    revealing locators.
11. the disposition remains provisional until the owner reviews the
    calibration outcome.

## risks and unknowns

- “settlement” may imply more consensus or finality than the interaction
  warrants.
- four predicates may reproduce the same taxonomy under gentler names.
- observer declarations can become ritual caveats unless omissions alter claim
  strength in practice.
- cross-status coupling may be difficult to distinguish from the analyst's
  narrative compression.
- one normal case and one boundary case test usability, not reliability or
  generality.
- the owner and analyst share substantial prior context; calibration is not an
  independent reading.
- a thin observer declaration may be insufficient where speaker attribution or
  reference resolution is itself the phenomenon.
- a complete observer instrument would exceed the needs and time boundary of
  the current study.
