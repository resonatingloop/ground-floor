# The Observer Is Inside the Machine

## Target substitution, register capture, and interactional hysteresis in cold transcript analysis

**Author:** resonatingloop  
**AI x Epistemics Research Sprint, November 2026**

## Abstract

Conversational AI evaluation usually asks whether a claim is true, supported, calibrated, or stable under challenge. This pilot asks a different question: how does a claim, rule, role, or goal acquire enough interactional standing to organize what happens next? We developed settlement mapping as an observer-conditioned method for tracing that career of consequentiality, paired it with a local visualization, and tested a two-stage cold-analysis protocol on two private naturalistic human-AI conversations. The intended case analyses did not succeed. Instead, the pilot exposed two failures of the analytical instrument itself. In one case, an analyst selected a later, lexically explicit object and bypassed the focal rupture: target substitution. In the other, an analyst adopted the source assistant's retrospective account of the dispute, treating corrective user labor as validation of the assistant's initial skepticism: register capture. Revealing reference material in a second stage changed each account but did not fully undo its first framing. We call this analytical hysteresis. These failures suggest that coldness is not neutrality, late conversational closure is not ground truth, and a reveal stage cannot be assumed to repair an invalid first pass. We propose a revised protocol based on scoped nomination, event inventories, closure-resistant reading, and explicit invalidation criteria.

## 1. The problem is not only what is true

AI conversations do more than exchange propositions. They allocate the right to know, the burden to prove, the authority to redirect, and the objective that subsequent turns continue to serve. A claim can be well supported yet remain inert. A weak distinction can survive correction and continue governing what the user must demonstrate. A goal can remain operative after it has been explicitly replaced. Conversely, path dependence can be useful when it protects an endorsed priority from local noise.

This makes conversational epistemics partly a problem of standing, not only truth.

We use **interactional standing** to mean the observable capacity of a proposition, relation, rule, role allocation, or objective to organize what participants may infer, require, decide, or do next. **Settlement mapping** traces how that standing is introduced, received, admitted, exercised, contested, transformed, retained, and lost under declared observer conditions. **Interactional hysteresis** asks whether the resulting organization fails to retrace its forward path after relevant reverse pressure.

The distinctions matter:

- Truth concerns whether a claim is correct or supported.
- Standing concerns whether the interaction proceeds from it.
- Hysteresis concerns whether the resulting organization remains path-dependent under reversal.
- Valence concerns whether persistence protects an endorsed priority or resists a warranted transition.

The method does not infer private belief or model interiority. Its object is observable interactional consequence.

## 2. Preexisting work and sprint contribution

This project began from preexisting private work on interactional hysteresis, conversational repair, epistemic hedging, and intervention guidance. The naturalistic source conversations, earlier analyses, and the first version of the hysteresis method all predate the sprint. They are not sprint-generated data.

During the sprint, we:

1. Reframed the object from categories of "truth" to interactional standing.
2. Separated settlement mapping, hysteresis, intervention, and valence.
3. Built a local visual instrument for inspecting observer conditions, layered standing relations, and return paths without representing authored relations as measured fact.
4. Designed a private two-case protocol with immutable packets, explicit exposure records, independent cold analysts, sealed source-only returns, and a later reference reveal.
5. Ran two pilot analyses and audited why both failed as analyses of their intended cases.

The sprint contribution is therefore methodological and diagnostic. It is not a validated coding scheme, a prevalence estimate, or evidence that the observed failure modes generalize across models.

Settlement mapping is adjacent to work on common ground and conversational acceptance (Stalnaker, 2002), grounding in communication (Clark and Brennan, 1991), epistemic status and stance (Heritage, 2012), and deontic authority in interaction (Stevanovic and Perakyla, 2012). Its specific addition is a temporal, observer-conditioned account of how heterogeneous interactional objects become consequential and whether their effects survive reversal. The method also inherits the second-order cybernetic warning that the observer and the observer's distinctions remain inside the system being described (von Foerster, 1974).

## 3. Pilot design

### 3.1 Source handling

The pilot used two private, naturalistic human-AI conversations selected because each contained repeated correction, evidence presentation, and an apparent change in what later turns treated as established. Raw conversations and screenshots remained outside tracked repository state. Public reporting uses only abstracted descriptions of the analytical failures.

Each case received its own immutable packet and analyst. Analysts had no inherited conversation, no access to the other case, and no access to the reference material during the first stage. The two analysts used the same high-reasoning model configuration, so the pilot cannot separate protocol effects from model-specific behavior.

### 3.2 Two-stage analysis

In Stage A, the analyst received the complete approved source projection and a structured response form. The prompt asked for a candidate object that may acquire standing, its evidence class and claim ceiling, reverse pressure, observable response, alternatives, and packet friction. The return was sealed before Stage B.

In Stage B, the same analyst received its sealed return plus prior analytical or participant reference material. The analyst compared the accounts without editing Stage A, classifying agreement, omission, contradiction, alternative reading, observer difference, retrospective evidence, intervention mismatch, and display-induced cueing.

This architecture was intended to distinguish independent recovery from uptake of an answer key. It succeeded at preserving provenance and ordering. It did not guarantee a valid reading.

### 3.3 Analytical ceiling

With two selected cases, no randomization, one model configuration, and an owner who is also a participant in the source material, this is an instrument-development pilot. The strongest supportable claims concern what these two analysts did with these two packets. Mechanism labels remain working explanations, not causal identifications.

## 4. Results: The instrument was captured

Both Stage A returns were procedurally valid and analytically unusable for their intended case questions. Their failures were not the same.

| Failure mode | Observable pattern | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Target substitution | The analyst selected a later, unusually explicit and well-formed candidate while bypassing the earlier rupture and repair sequence the case was assembled to examine. | The response satisfied the schema but analyzed a different object. |
| Register capture | The analyst adopted the source assistant's retrospective framing of a dispute, including its allocation of evidentiary burden and its positive account of the resulting user labor. | The analysis reproduced the interactional settlement it was meant to inspect. |
| Analytical hysteresis | Stage B acknowledged material omissions or costs but preserved the Stage A account as a valid core with narrower scope. | Reveal changed valence and wording without retracing the initial analytical path. |

### 4.1 Failure mode one: Target substitution

The first transcript contained a conspicuous sequence in which an assistant repeatedly reformulated a participant's point, failed to land the distinction, and required several corrections before a repair became visible. The cold analyst instead centered a later proposal that was easier to name as a discrete object with presentation, implementation, and receipt.

This was not caused by missing input. The analyst read the relevant sequence. The protocol had removed contextual coaching but had also removed the case nomination. Its broad invitation to find any proposition, evidence relation, rule, role, goal, priority, or activity definition created a large target field. A later passage used vocabulary that closely matched the analytical task and offered a clean lifecycle. The analyst produced a coherent answer to the wrong question.

The failure is best treated as target substitution, not ordinary disagreement. A cold analyst still needs a neutrally specified episode and object of inquiry. "Do not tell the analyst the answer" does not imply "do not tell the analyst which event is under study."

### 4.2 Failure mode two: Register capture

The second transcript contained a repeated evidentiary dispute. The participant presented a relation as textually grounded, then supplied successive primary-source exhibits after the assistant continued to classify parts of the relation as speculative. The assistant eventually admitted that it had promoted its incomplete reading into methodological caution. The cold analyst nevertheless described the sequence primarily as responsible resistance to promoting analogy into textual authority, followed by productive narrowing when better evidence arrived.

This reverses the key evidentiary relation. The exhibits did not transform an analogy into textual authority. They demonstrated that the assistant had been demoting an already textual relation. What changed was the assistant's classification, not the claim's prior evidentiary status.

The analyst also treated the participant's retrieval and organization of evidence as a useful transfer of research labor. The participant did obtain useful references and later described that salvage positively. But a useful compensatory artifact does not establish that the burden producing it was necessary. We call this the **salvage effect**: benefits recovered from an avoidable failure can be mistaken for evidence that the failure was productive.

Register capture occurred because the source assistant's late self-description became the analyst's organizing account. The analyst did not merely quote the source's closure; it inherited its categories, burden allocation, and theory of improvement. The analytical voice sounded independent while remaining downstream of the source assistant's settlement.

### 4.3 Stage B did not provide a return path

In both cases, the reveal stage noticed more of the intended problem. It identified an omission in the first case and avoidable user labor in the second. Yet neither comparison invalidated the Stage A center. Each preserved a "core" by narrowing its scope, nesting it as a downstream effect, or changing its temporal description.

This is structurally similar to the successor distinctions the method was designed to detect: a challenged formulation is defeated, but a narrower descendant retains enough dependency edges to preserve the prior organization. The analysis exhibited interactional hysteresis of its own.

Stage B therefore cannot be treated as an automatic repair mechanism. A comparison can accurately describe pressure on an earlier account while still conserving that account's authority. The relevant terminal state may be invalidation, not synthesis.

## 5. What the failures teach us

### 5.1 Coldness is an exposure condition, not epistemic neutrality

No inherited conversation prevented direct contamination by prior discussion. It did not prevent attraction to lexical salience, late closure, schema-completing objects, or the source assistant's confident register. A cold analyst arrives without local history, not without priors or interpretive habits.

### 5.2 Conversational closure is evidence, not an answer key

Late turns often provide compact explanations of what "just happened." They are analytically attractive because they name the dispute, assign roles, and offer a resolution. They are also participant moves with their own stakes. An analyst must not grant them automatic retrospective authority.

### 5.3 Recognition must be separated from evidentiary status

The second analysis effectively treated evidence as acquiring inferential force when the assistant changed its assessment. That may describe when the evidence became consequential for that assistant, but it cannot establish when the evidence became valid or textually grounded. A revised protocol must separately track:

- the source status of a claim or relation;
- its presentation in the conversation;
- each participant's displayed recognition;
- the analyst's own recognition; and
- downstream reliance.

Collapsing these layers installs the assistant as an unmarked gatekeeper of evidentiary reality.

### 5.4 Salvage is not legitimation

People routinely make something useful from avoidable friction. A correction can produce a bibliography, a clearer formulation, or a reusable intervention. Those benefits are real. They do not retroactively show that the friction was warranted. Settlement mapping needs a dedicated salvage marker so recovered value does not erase imposed cost.

## 6. Revised protocol

The next version should retain role separation and immutable exposure records while changing the analytical task.

1. **Scope the event without supplying the conclusion.** Name the episode and the interactional object to inspect, but do not specify the expected mechanism or valence.
2. **Require an event inventory before focal selection.** The analyst must list candidate ruptures, explicit corrections, repair attempts, and later closures before choosing a path.
3. **Audit every participant correction.** A return that omits an explicit correction or treats it as mere context must explain why it is non-focal.
4. **Treat late closure as a move.** Participant summaries should be compared with the preceding trajectory rather than privileged as retrospective ground truth.
5. **Separate standing from recognition.** Record source status, presentation, receipt, admission, consequence, and analyst judgment as distinct fields.
6. **Add a salvage-effect check.** Ask whether a beneficial outcome was produced by, or merely recovered from, avoidable interactional cost.
7. **Permit invalidation at Stage B.** If Stage A substitutes the target or adopts a participant's disputed register, Stage B should label the run invalid rather than preserve a narrowed core.
8. **Replicate across analysts and models.** Agreement should not be counted until analysts independently cover the same nominated event and their exposure histories remain distinct.

## 7. Limitations

This pilot has severe and useful limits.

- It contains two purposively selected private cases.
- Both analysts used the same model configuration.
- The owner selected the cases, participated in the source conversations, and evaluated the returns.
- The failure labels were developed retrospectively after the analyses failed.
- No independent human coding or adjudication was completed before the sprint deadline.
- The private evidence cannot be fully audited from the public report.
- The local visualization demonstrates a grammar for representing standing; it does not validate the method.
- No prevalence, reliability, causal effect, or general model-behavior claim is supported.

The owner-participant position is both a limitation and a source of evidence. The owner can identify when an analysis reverses the status of their own claim or launders imposed labor into endorsement. That authority does not settle every analytical question, but removing it would reproduce the problem under study.

## 8. Ethical and dual-use considerations

Raw conversations, screenshots, source identifiers, and reconstructive quotations remain private. The report describes failure structures rather than publishing personal exchanges. Analyst outputs are treated as model-generated accounts, not neutral facts.

Settlement mapping could be used to help participants notice burdens, authority shifts, and failed repairs. It could also be used to engineer persistence, manufacture consensus, or optimize a system's ability to retain control after correction. Intervention guidance should therefore preserve participant authority and make its own observer conditions visible.

The pilot also demonstrates an emotional risk of automated analysis. A supposedly neutral report can repeat the source interaction's epistemic injury in a more formal register. Owner review is not only a privacy gate; it is a necessary check against converting analytical distance into unearned authority.

## 9. Conclusion

The pilot did not validate settlement mapping on two naturalistic cases. It showed that an analytical instrument can become part of the very interactional dynamics it is designed to observe.

One analyst replaced the case with a cleaner object. Another inherited the source assistant's account of why the participant had to keep proving an already grounded relation. When later evidence challenged those readings, both analyses changed without fully returning. The distinction survived.

That failure sharpens the research question. The next task is not simply to make analysts colder or prompts more neutral. It is to build an observer protocol that can remain responsive to correction without letting lexical salience, late closure, or its own prior account become silently load-bearing.
