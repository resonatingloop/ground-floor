We developed settlement mapping, an observer-conditioned method for tracing how claims, evidence relations, roles, rules, and goals become consequential in human-AI conversation. A two-case cold-analysis pilot did not validate the method. Instead, it exposed two failures of the analytical instrument: target substitution, where an analyst selected a later, cleaner object and bypassed the focal rupture; and register capture, where an analyst adopted the source assistant's account of a dispute and treated corrective user labor as validation of the assistant's skepticism. A second-stage reference reveal narrowed both accounts but did not fully undo their first framing, producing analytical hysteresis. The result is a revised protocol: nominate the event without supplying the answer, require an event inventory, treat late conversational closure as evidence rather than ground truth, separate evidentiary status from assistant recognition, test for salvage effects, and invalidate rather than synthesize captured first passes.
